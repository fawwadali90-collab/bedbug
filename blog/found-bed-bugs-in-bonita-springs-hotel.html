Found Bed Bugs in a Bonita Springs Hotel? Do This

October 9, 2026 · Naples Bed Bug Treatment

Finding bed bugs in your Bonita Springs hotel room is stressful — but what you do in the next hour matters more than the discovery itself. Here's a calm, step-by-step plan for the room, your belongings, the hotel conversation, and protecting your home.

Travel Emergency

First, Don't Panic — and Don't Scatter

Finding bed bugs in a hotel room — beachfront resort or budget stop along US-41 — is unsettling. But your reaction in the next hour matters most. Bed bugs don't reflect on your cleanliness, and finding them doesn't mean you'll bring them home. You need a calm, methodical plan.

Bonita Springs sits in a busy travel corridor between Naples and Fort Myers. Bed bugs can show up in any hotel at any price point — they travel in luggage, not dirt, and no hotel is immune. Here's the playbook: securing the room, protecting your belongings, handling the hotel conversation, and keeping bugs from following you home.

The Golden Rule of Hotel Bed Bugs

Contain, don't scatter. Most damage after finding bed bugs comes from frantic reactions — throwing clothes around, dragging luggage through the hotel, tossing everything into the car. Slow, deliberate containment keeps a one-room problem from becoming a whole-house problem.

In the Room

Step-by-Step: Securing Your Room and Your Belongings

Document What You Found

Take clear photos of any live bugs, spots, or shed skins — including the mattress seams and headboard area. Note the room number, date, and time. Photos help with the hotel conversation and any later claim.

Isolate Your Belongings

Move luggage into the bathroom — bed bugs rarely harbor on cold tile — and seal dirty laundry in tied bags. Don't set your suitcase on the bed, the chair, or near the nightstand.

Do a Quick Luggage Check

Before leaving, inspect suitcase seams, pockets, and wheels with your phone flashlight — live bugs, tiny white eggs, dark spots. It won't guarantee the bag is clear, but it catches obvious hitchhikers.

Do Not Scatter

Resist the urge to throw clothes around the room or drag everything into the hallway. Panicked movement is how bed bugs end up in places they weren't — including deeper into your own belongings.

Before You Walk In

Protecting Your Home Before You Return to Naples

The most dangerous moment is not the hotel room — it is when your suitcase crosses your home's threshold. Plan your arrival before you get there.

Stop in the garage, not the bedroom. Do not carry luggage straight to the bedroom and unpack. Take everything to the garage or laundry room first and inspect the suitcase in good light — seams, zippers, pockets, wheels, and lining.

Heat is your best weapon at home. Everything that can go in the dryer should go in on the hottest setting the fabric can handle, for at least 30 minutes. Dry heat kills bed bugs at every life stage, including eggs — even clothes you did not wear, if they were in the room. Items that cannot be dried deserve a careful visual inspection and isolation in sealed bags.

Vacuum the suitcase itself. Run a crevice tool along every seam and pocket, then empty the vacuum outside into a sealed bag. Store the suitcase in the garage or a storage area — not under the bed or in the closet.

Watch for signs over the coming weeks. Monitor your bedroom for rust-colored spots on sheets, shed skins along mattress seams, and new overnight bite marks. If anything shows up, schedule a professional inspection promptly — early infestations are far simpler to resolve.

Your Arrival Checklist

Luggage stays in the garage. Inspect every seam in good light. Hot dryer for at least 30 minutes for all fabrics. Vacuum the suitcase, empty the vacuum outside. Store luggage away from bedrooms. Watch for signs for several weeks.

Getting Help

How to Talk to the Hotel (and What to Ask For)

Report It Promptly and Politely

Go to the front desk — don't just leave a note at checkout. State the facts: what you found, where, and show your photos. A good hotel wants to know; an unreported room stays in rotation.

Ask for a Room Change, Not Next Door

Request a room that does not share a wall with the affected one — ideally on a different floor or wing. Bed bugs travel through wall voids, so the adjacent room is the worst alternative.

Ask What They Will Do About the Room

A responsible hotel takes the room out of service and has it professionally inspected. You are entitled to ask whether that will happen.

Ask About Laundry Help

Many hotels will run your clothes through their commercial dryers at high heat as a courtesy. It never hurts to ask — it is one of the most effective decontamination steps available.

Stay Vigilant

Watching for Hitchhikers After You Get Home

Check Your Bed Weekly

For the next month or so, pull back the sheets and check mattress seams, piping, and the headboard with a flashlight. Early signs — a few dark spots, a shed skin — are easy to miss if you're not looking.

Keep Luggage Out of the Bedroom

Store the trip's suitcase in the garage or a storage area, not the bedroom closet. If any hitchhikers survived your arrival routine, distance from the bed limits what they can do.

Do Not Move Furniture Around

If you suspect bed bugs made it home, don't move the bed or start sleeping in another room — that just gives the bugs a new location to colonize. Stay put and get an inspection.

Know When to Call a Professional

Any confirmed sign — a live bug, clear droppings, shed skins — means it's time for a professional inspection. New bite marks for several mornings running after a trip? Don't wait for visual proof. Our emergency bed bug treatment is built for urgent post-travel situations.
