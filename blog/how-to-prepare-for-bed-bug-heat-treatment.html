How to Prepare for Bed Bug Heat Treatment

October 9, 2026

Heat treatment day goes smoothly when the prep is done right. This room-by-room guide covers what to set aside, what to wash, and how to avoid the mistakes that slow crews down.

Before Treatment Day

Why Preparation Matters for Heat Treatment

Heat treatment is the most hands-off bed bug solution available — one visit, no chemicals, and the crew handles the technical work. But it is not zero-effort on your side. Good preparation does three things: it protects your heat-sensitive belongings, it lets warm air circulate into every corner so no harborage stays cool, and it keeps the crew on schedule instead of working around obstacles.

Preparation is simpler than most people expect: you do not need to bag your entire home, and your inspection includes a walkthrough of exactly what your space needs. This post expands that checklist into a room-by-room guide, so treatment day starts on time and finishes without surprises.

Step One

Set Aside Heat-Sensitive Items

Walk through each treated room and gather anything that should not be exposed to high heat — getting a head start the evening before keeps the morning smooth. Set these items aside in an untreated area — a garage, a car trunk, or a room that is not part of the treatment zone:

  • Candles and wax items — they will melt. Check decorative shelves and bathrooms.
  • Aerosol cans and pressurized containers — hairspray, spray paint, cleaners, and similar products under sinks and in garages.
  • Certain medications — anything labeled to store at room temperature or below, especially in bathroom cabinets.
  • Houseplants — most do not tolerate sustained high heat. Move them out for the day.
  • Delicate electronics and valuables — ask the crew about anything you are unsure of, such as fine artwork or instruments.

When in doubt, set it aside and ask. The arrival walkthrough exists precisely to catch the items people forget — a candle on a high shelf, a can of sunscreen in a beach bag — so flag anything questionable at the door.

Step Two

Laundry: Wash, Dry, and Bag It

Bedding, clothing, and curtains near sleeping areas can harbor bed bugs and eggs, and the dryer is your best tool against them. Wash bedding and clothing from affected rooms, then run everything through a hot dryer cycle — sustained dryer heat kills all life stages. Once dry, seal items in clean bags or bins and keep them sealed until treatment is finished.

Two rules make laundry prep effective instead of risky. First, do not redistribute: bag items in the room where you collected them rather than carrying armloads of potentially infested laundry through clean rooms. Second, treat the laundry area as part of the workflow — empty hampers near beds go through the same wash-and-bag routine. Clean, bagged laundry waiting in sealed bags is one less thing the crew has to work around, and it means you come home to fresh bedding instead of a chore.

Step Three

Declutter Without Spreading Bed Bugs

Heat needs airflow. Warm air must reach into every corner, behind every piece of furniture, and inside every closet — and clutter is what blocks it. The goal is not a spotless home; it is clear floors and accessible walls. Pick up items from bedroom floors, clear the tops of dressers near beds, and make sure the crew can walk freely around every piece of furniture being treated.

The critical rule while decluttering: work room by room and do not move items between rooms. Carrying a stack of magazines from an infested bedroom to the living room "to get it out of the way" is exactly how bed bugs colonize new territory. Bag as you go and keep bags sealed. If a room is heavily cluttered, mention it during the inspection so the plan can include extra time.

The One Mistake to Avoid

Do not move infested furniture out of the room before treatment. Heat treats furniture in place, and dragging it through the home spreads bed bugs to every hallway and room it passes. Leave it where it is — the crew will handle it.

Step Four

Give the Crew Full Access

On treatment day, the crew places heaters, high-volume fans, and wireless temperature sensors throughout the space — and every one of those needs a clear spot. Pull beds and large furniture a short distance from the walls so air circulates behind them. Open closet doors and leave drawers slightly ajar so heat penetrates inside. Make sure every room being treated is unlocked and accessible, including spare bedrooms and storage areas you rarely enter.

If your home has an attic access, a locked lanai storage area, or a guest house on the property, confirm with the crew whether those spaces are in scope. Clear a path from the entrance to the work areas — heaters and fans are bulky. A few minutes of clearing hallways the night before saves real time on treatment morning.

Step Five

Plan for People, Pets, and Plants

Everyone needs to be out of the treated space while temperatures are elevated — family members, dogs, cats, birds, reptiles, and houseplants. Plan to be away for the better part of the day. Most Naples clients make a day of it: the beach, a long lunch on Fifth Avenue, errands that have been waiting. Arrange pet care in advance, whether that means a friend, a daycare, or a cool car-free plan — pets cannot wait in a vehicle in the Florida heat.

Before you leave, do a final sweep: stove and oven off, and any items you set aside are out of the treatment zone. Leave a key or access instructions as arranged with the crew. You will get a call with a specific return time — and when you walk back in, the home is back to a comfortable temperature with nothing to air out and no residue to worry about.

Naples Specifics

Condo and HOA Logistics

Naples condos add a layer of logistics worth handling ahead of time. Many buildings require elevator reservations for moving equipment, restrict loading-dock hours, or need advance notice before work begins in a unit. Check with your property manager a few days before treatment: reserve the elevator window, confirm where the crew can park and stage equipment, and ask about any building forms or notifications the HOA requires.

It is also courteous — and often required — to let the HOA know treatment is scheduled, since shared walls may mean neighboring units get a courtesy inspection. Handling building logistics in advance means treatment day starts on time. A company that works in Naples condos regularly will remind you of these steps, but the reservations themselves are yours to make.
