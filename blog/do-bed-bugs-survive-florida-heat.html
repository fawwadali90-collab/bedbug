Do Bed Bugs Survive Florida Heat?

October 9, 2026 · Naples Bed Bug Treatment

Southwest Florida is hot most of the year — so shouldn't the heat kill bed bugs on its own? The answer is no, and understanding why explains a lot about how bed bugs thrive in Naples-area homes.

Heat and Bed Bugs

The Short Answer: No — Florida Weather Can't Do the Job

It is one of the most common questions we hear in Southwest Florida: with summer days that feel like stepping into an oven, shouldn't the heat kill bed bugs on its own? The honest answer is no. Florida's outdoor heat — even at the peak of August in Naples — never reaches the sustained, penetrating temperatures needed to kill bed bugs and their eggs. And bed bugs are experts at avoiding the heat that does exist.

Killing bed bugs with heat means raising the temperature inside the mattress, inside the walls, and inside the furniture where the bugs actually hide — to a lethal level, held long enough that every life stage dies, including the eggs. A hot Florida afternoon might make your garage uncomfortable, but the inside of your air-conditioned bedroom wall is perfectly comfortable for a bed bug. The heat they tolerate and the heat that kills them are very different things.

The One Thing to Remember

Bed bugs die from heat only when it is hot enough, everywhere they hide, for long enough — all three at once. A Florida summer delivers none of them reliably. Professional heat treatment delivers all three by design.

Climate Reality

Why Southwest Florida Is Ideal for Bed Bugs Year-Round

No Real Winter to Slow Them Down

Bed bugs are most active in warm conditions, and Naples rarely sees the sustained cold that would pause their breeding cycle. Infestations here keep growing straight through January.

Air Conditioning Creates Perfect Hiding Spots

Bed bugs prefer the same temperatures humans find comfortable. A home cooled to the mid-70s is their ideal environment — warm enough to stay active and breed, never hot enough to be harmed.

Year-Round Tourism Means Year-Round Introductions

Snowbird season, spring break, summer visitors — Naples hosts travelers every month of the year, and every arrival is a potential new introduction via hotels, rentals, and cruise-adjacent stays.

Condos and Shared Walls Multiply the Effect

Much of Naples lives in condos and multi-unit buildings, where bed bugs move between units through wall voids and shared infrastructure. Warm weather plus dense housing lets one introduction spread quietly.

Myth Vs Reality

Common Myths About Heat and Bed Bugs, Debunked

"My Car Gets Hot Enough to Kill Them"

A closed car in a Naples parking lot gets extremely hot — but the heat is wildly uneven. The dashboard may scorch while the inside of a suitcase in the trunk stays merely warm, and bugs tucked into seams or under seats survive in the cooler pockets.

"I'll Just Leave the House Un-Air-Conditioned"

Turning off the AC for a weekend does not come close. Interior walls, closets, and furniture insulate bed bugs from outdoor heat, and nighttime temperatures drop back into their comfort zone.

"The Attic or Garage Will Bake Them Out"

Bed bugs do not live in your attic or garage — they live within a few feet of where you sleep. Heating spaces they don't occupy accomplishes nothing.

"Summer Is When They Die Off"

The opposite is true here. Summer is peak travel season and peak breeding season. In Southwest Florida, bed bug activity accelerates in summer — warmth plus constant introductions drives population growth.

Bed Bug Behavior

Where Bed Bugs Hide When It Gets Hot

Bed bugs are not passive victims of temperature — they actively seek out the conditions they prefer. When outdoor temperatures climb, they simply stay where it is comfortable: inside your air-conditioned bedroom. Their entire survival strategy is built around staying close to a sleeping human, which means staying inside the climate-controlled envelope of your home.

Their favorite hiding spots are all naturally insulated against outdoor heat: inside a mattress or box spring, behind a headboard, in nightstand joints, along baseboards, inside wall voids and electrical outlets. The inside of a wall void in August is not an oven — it is a shaded, stable cavity. Eggs are the hardest part of any infestation to eliminate precisely because they are glued into cracks in these sheltered spots.

This hiding behavior is exactly what professional heat treatment defeats. Technicians introduce powerful heaters and circulation fans that push sustained, lethal heat into every sheltered space — and wireless sensors verify that the inside of the mattress, the wall voids, and the furniture all actually reach the kill temperature.

Think Like a Bed Bug

When it is hot outside, bed bugs do exactly what you do: they stay inside where the air conditioning is. Any strategy that relies on Florida's outdoor heat is fighting the battle in a place the bugs are not.

The Real Solution

What Actually Kills Bed Bugs With Heat

Temperature: Lethal, Not Just Warm

Technicians raise treated rooms well above what bed bugs can tolerate — far beyond any temperature your home reaches naturally, even in August. This is a carefully controlled thermal environment, not just "uncomfortably warm."

Penetration: Into Walls and Furniture

Industrial heaters and high-volume fans force hot air into mattresses, box springs, wall voids, and furniture joints — the sheltered spots where bed bugs and eggs wait out a hot day. Sensors confirm the heat actually arrives where the bugs are.

Time: Held Long Enough to Finish the Job

The lethal temperature is maintained for hours, not minutes, because eggs are more heat-resistant than adults. Holding the heat is what makes one visit enough.

Verification: Sensors, Not Guesswork

Wireless temperature sensors placed throughout the space give real readings from inside furniture and wall voids. Treatment is finished when the data confirms every monitored point reached the kill temperature — not when the clock runs out.

Be Proactive

What You Can Do This Summer Besides Hope for Heat

Florida's heat will not solve a bed bug problem — but these habits will keep one from starting.

Inspect After Every Trip

Summer travel is the top introduction route. After any hotel stay, inspect luggage in the garage before it enters the house, and run washables through a hot dryer cycle.

Encase Your Mattresses

Bed bug-proof encasements remove the bugs' favorite harborage and make any future inspection faster and more reliable.

Know the Early Signs

Rust-colored spots on sheets, shed skins along mattress seams, and clustered overnight bites are the classic early warnings. Catching an infestation in its first weeks makes treatment simpler.

Consider a Prevention Plan

A recurring bed bug prevention plan with scheduled professional inspections is built for this climate — year-round vigilance for a pest that never takes a season off here.
